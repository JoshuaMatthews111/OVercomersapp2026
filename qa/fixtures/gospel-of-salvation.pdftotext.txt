The Gospel of Salvation JOSHUA MATTHEWS A Scripture-centered book on what salvation is, what Christ accomplished, what the believer has received, and how the gospel must be carried with power. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version. Contents INTRODUCTION What Is the Gospel of Salvation? CHAPTER 1 What Happened to Man? CHAPTER 2 The Savior and the Messiah CHAPTER 3 Salvation Is Found in Christ Alone CHAPTER 4 The Cross and the Blood of Jesus CHAPTER 5 Repentance, Living Faith, and Confession CHAPTER 6 Born of Water and of the Spirit CHAPTER 7 A New Creature and the Gift of Righteousness CHAPTER 8 No Condemnation, Confession, and Cleansing CHAPTER 9 Grace: Favor, Power, and Enablement CHAPTER 10 Delivered from Darkness into His Kingdom CHAPTER 11 Eternal Life and a Spirit-Led Life CHAPTER 12 The Gospel Must Reach You, Your House, and the World CONCLUSION Keep Christ at the Center SHARING GUIDE Carry the Gospel Clearly INTRODUCTION What Is the Gospel of Salvation? I know many of you may be wondering what we really mean when we say the gospel of salvation. We have heard the word gospel so many times that it can become familiar before it ever becomes clear. The gospel is not a church slogan, and salvation is not simply a religious word. Gospel means good news. It is the announcement that God has acted in Jesus Christ to rescue man from sin, death, darkness, condemnation, and separation from God, and to bring him into new life. But before we go any further, we must put the gospel in front of everything. Salvation is not first about what man promises God. It is about what God has done for man in Christ. Paul said that the message he delivered first was that Christ died for our sins, that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures. “For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures.” 1 CORINTHIANS 15:3-4 That is our center. Christ died. Christ was buried. Christ rose. Everything else in this book explains what those events mean for the person who believes. We are going to talk about sin, but we will keep the Savior in front. We are going to talk about repentance, but we will keep grace in front. We are going to talk about a changed life, but we will never confuse the fruit of salvation with the price of salvation. Salvation begins with God, comes through Christ, is received by a living faith, and produces a new life. It reaches the spirit of man, his standing before God, his relationship with the Father, his freedom from darkness, his mind, his conduct, his house, and eventually the way he carries Christ to others. So as you read, do not read this like a list of doctrines to memorize. Read it as the story of what God has done to bring man back to Himself. The gospel is not cold information. It is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth. “For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth.” ROMANS 1:16 DEGREE AND DECLARE Christ is the center of my salvation. I will not build my confidence on religion, emotion, family tradition, or my own works. I receive the good news of what God has done for me in Jesus Christ. • • • CHAPTER ONE What Happened to Man? You will never treasure the rescue until you understand the danger. If a man thinks his problem is only that he needs to improve a few habits, then he will look for a better program. But if he sees what sin actually did to humanity, then the coming of a Savior becomes good news. Created in the Image and Glory of God The story does not begin with sin. It begins with purpose. God created man in His own image and likeness and gave him dominion. Adam was not created for empty religion. He was created for fellowship with God and life under God’s government. He was to be fruitful, multiply, subdue the earth, and rule under the authority of the One who made him. “So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.” GENESIS 1:27 Man was designed to reflect God. This is why Romans 3:23 does not only say that all have sinned; it says all have come short of the glory of God. Sin is more than a list of wrong actions. It is the fall of man from the glory and purpose for which he was made. The Fall Was More Than a Bad Decision In Genesis 3, man disobeyed God, shame entered, hiding entered, death entered, and the human family came under the power of sin. Paul later explains that by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin, and so death passed upon all men. The problem spread beyond Adam because humanity itself had fallen. “Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned.” ROMANS 5:12 This means every person needs salvation. It does not matter whether sin is public or hidden, whether a person grew up in church or never entered one. The Bible brings all men to the same conclusion: we have fallen short, and our own righteousness cannot become the basis on which God accepts us. “But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags.” ISAIAH 64:6 Isaiah is not saying that the righteousness God gives is filthy. He is exposing the inability of self-righteous man to cleanse himself before a holy God. Our own record cannot save us. Our own goodness cannot erase sin. The wages of sin is death. “For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.” ROMANS 6:23 Notice the contrast: wages and gift. Sin pays a wage. God gives a gift. Salvation begins when man stops pretending that he can pay his own debt and receives what God has provided through Jesus Christ. And this is where hope enters. The same glory man fell short of is the glory God is restoring in His sons. Hebrews says Christ is bringing many sons unto glory. Salvation is not God throwing away His original purpose for man. It is God restoring man to Himself through Christ. DEGREE AND DECLARE I will not hide my need for a Savior. I have fallen short of the glory of God, but Christ came for me. I receive mercy, and I receive the life God gives through Jesus Christ. • • • CHAPTER TWO The Savior and the Messiah Once we understand man’s need, the next question is simple: Who can save him? The answer of the gospel is not a program, a philosophy, or a better religious system. The answer is a Person: Jesus Christ. What Does Messiah Mean? The word Messiah comes from the Hebrew word mashiach, meaning the anointed one. The Greek word Christos gives us the word Christ and carries the same idea: the Anointed One. Christ is not Jesus’ last name. It is a title. When we say Jesus Christ, we are declaring Jesus the promised Anointed One, the One God promised through the prophets and sent to fulfill His redemptive purpose. The God who saves is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The promise of salvation did not begin in the New Testament. God told Abraham that through him all families of the earth would be blessed. Through Israel came the covenants, the prophets, the Scriptures, and according to the flesh, the Messiah. Jesus Himself said, “salvation is of the Jews.” “And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.” MATTHEW 1:21 His name announces His mission. Jesus comes from the Hebrew name Yeshua, connected with the truth that the LORD saves. He did not come merely to make men feel inspired. He came to save His people from their sins. The Eternal Word Became Flesh We must also settle who Jesus is. He was not merely a prophet who needed a Savior himself. John takes us before Bethlehem and says, “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” Then he says that the Word was made flesh. The eternal Son entered our human condition without ceasing to be who He was. “And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.” JOHN 1:14 The Father sent Him, anointed Him, and publicly revealed Him as the promised Messiah. Peter preached that God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power, and that He went about doing good and healing all that were oppressed of the devil. The anointing did not make Jesus divine; the eternal Word already was God. The anointing revealed and empowered His messianic mission in the flesh. “How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him.” ACTS 10:38 Only the sinless Son could stand in our place. He was truly man, so He could die our death; and He was the eternal Son, so death could not master Him. Hebrews says that through death He destroyed him that had the power of death, that is, the devil, and delivered those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. This is why Jesus is not simply one option among religious teachers. He is the Savior because of who He is and what He accomplished. DEGREE AND DECLARE Jesus is the Christ, the promised Messiah, the eternal Word made flesh. He is my Savior and Lord. I trust the One whom the Father sent, anointed, raised, and exalted. • • • CHAPTER THREE Salvation Is Found in Christ Alone This chapter is where many religious assumptions have to be laid down. Salvation is not going to church alone. Salvation is not because water touched you when you were a baby. Salvation is not because you have a praying mother or grandmother. Salvation is not because there is a Bible in your bedroom. Salvation is not because you try to be a good person. None of these things can substitute for Jesus Christ. A church family matters. Prayer matters. The Bible matters. Baptism matters. Good works matter in the life of a believer. But none of them can become another Savior. The question is not, “What religious things are around me?” The question is, “Have I received the Son?” “Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.” ACTS 4:12 Peter spoke those words before the religious council that had rejected Jesus. He did not say there were many roads and Jesus was his preferred road. He declared that salvation is found in one Name. Grace Removes Boasting “For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.” EPHESIANS 2:8-9 Grace is the source, and faith receives what grace has provided. Faith is not a payment. Faith does not give God a reason to owe us salvation. The finished work is Christ’s. This is why boasting is excluded. No man will stand before God and say, “I saved myself because I was moral enough.” Our own righteousness cannot purchase a right standing with God. Isaiah said all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags. Paul said he refused to stand in his own righteousness and desired the righteousness which is through the faith of Christ. Salvation humbles us because it strips away every false foundation. Good Works Are Fruit, Not Price Good works have a place, but they come after grace. Ephesians 2:10 follows immediately after verses 8 and 9. We are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works. We are not saved by good works, but the person who has truly received new life will begin to walk in what God prepared. The gospel therefore rejects two errors at the same time. It rejects legalism, which tries to earn what Christ gives freely. And it rejects a dead profession that wants the gift while refusing the Giver. Living faith receives Christ, and receiving Christ begins to change the life. “And this is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.” 1 JOHN 5:11-12 Life is in the Son. That is why we keep the gospel in front. We are not trying to produce people who trust church attendance, family heritage, a childhood ceremony, or good intentions. We are calling people to Jesus Christ. DEGREE AND DECLARE My confidence is in Christ alone. I do not trust my works, my family background, a religious ceremony, or my own goodness to save me. I have the Son, and in Him I have life. • • • CHAPTER FOUR The Cross and the Blood of Jesus We cannot preach salvation and hurry past the blood. The blood of Jesus is not a religious phrase to repeat without understanding. It speaks of the life of Christ poured out, the covenant God established, the price of redemption, the cleansing of sin, and the way we have been brought near to God. Life Is in the Blood “For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls.” LEVITICUS 17:11 Long before Calvary, God was teaching Israel that sin brings death and that atonement involves life given in the place of another. The sacrifices could not finally remove sin, but they were shadows pointing forward to Christ. At Passover, the blood of the lamb was placed on the doorposts, and judgment passed over the house. In the tabernacle, blood was brought before God. In the prophets, a suffering Servant was promised. Then John the Baptist saw Jesus and cried, “Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.” Without Shedding of Blood “And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.” HEBREWS 9:22 Forgiveness is free to us, but it was not cheap. God did not pretend sin did not matter. Christ bore our sins in His own body on the tree. He gave His life. The cross is substitution: the innocent One taking the place of the guilty so that the guilty may receive mercy and new life. “In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace.” EPHESIANS 1:7 Redemption means release at a price. We were not bought with silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. The blood tells you that your freedom cost something. It also tells you the price has been paid. The Blood Still Speaks Hebrews says we have come to “the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.” Abel’s blood cried from the ground for justice. The blood of Jesus speaks a better word over the believer: mercy, covenant, cleansing, access, reconciliation. “But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.” 1 JOHN 1:7 The blood is not a magic formula. Its power is in the finished sacrifice of Christ to which it refers. We do not use the phrase “the blood” as superstition. We understand what heaven says happened at Calvary. Christ gave Himself. A covenant was established. Sin was judged. Redemption was purchased. The accuser has been answered. Revelation says the saints overcame by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony. The believer stands in what Christ accomplished and bears witness to it. DEGREE AND DECLARE I am redeemed through the blood of Jesus. His blood speaks mercy over me, cleanses me as I walk in the light, and gives me confidence to draw near to God. I will never treat the cross as a small thing. • • • CHAPTER FIVE Repentance, Living Faith, and Confession The gospel calls for a response. Grace does not make response unnecessary; grace makes response possible. Jesus came preaching, “Repent ye, and believe the gospel.” Paul summarized his ministry as “repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ.” Repentance Is a Turn Repentance begins with a change of mind, but it does not end in the mind. If I truly change my mind about God, sin, and who has the right to rule my life, my direction begins to change. Acts says men should repent, turn to God, and do works meet for repentance. The works do not purchase forgiveness; they reveal that the turn is real. “Testifying both to the Jews, and also to the Greeks, repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ.” ACTS 20:21 Faith Must Be Living You cannot simply think, in a detached mental way, “I know Christ died,” and assume that intellectual information alone is the living faith Scripture describes. James says that even devils believe that God is one, and tremble. Saving faith receives Christ, relies upon Him, and begins to express itself in obedience. This is why James can say faith without works is dead without contradicting Paul. Paul rejects works as the price of salvation. James rejects a so-called faith that has no living expression. Works do not make faith alive; living faith produces works because life is present. The Heart Believes and the Mouth Gives Expression “For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.” ROMANS 10:10 The heart believes, and the mouth gives expression to what the heart has received. Confession is not an empty formula that saves by pronunciation. It is the outward expression of an inward faith. When a man confesses Jesus as Lord, he is not only saying a religious sentence; he is acknowledging Christ’s right to rule his life. Romans 10 joins the heart and the mouth because salvation reaches the whole person. The heart trusts the risen Christ, and the mouth is no longer ashamed to identify with Him. “That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.” ROMANS 10:9 A living faith will keep expressing itself after the first confession. It will show up in prayer, obedience, baptism, forgiveness, worship, generosity, holiness, and the willingness to be identified with Jesus before others. DEGREE AND DECLARE I turn toward God with my whole heart. I believe in the risen Christ, and my mouth gives expression to what my heart has received. My faith is alive, and my life will increasingly agree with what I believe. • • • CHAPTER SIX Born of Water and of the Spirit Jesus did not tell Nicodemus that he needed to improve his religion. Nicodemus was already religious, educated, and respected. Jesus went deeper and said, “Ye must be born again.” Salvation is not cosmetic improvement. It is life from above. “Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.” JOHN 3:3 The word translated “again” can carry the sense of “from above.” The new birth is not a second attempt at the old life. God gives life by His Spirit. Water and Spirit “Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.” JOHN 3:5 Christians have understood the “water” language in more than one way. Some see a direct reference to baptism. Others hear an echo of Ezekiel 36, where God promised cleansing water together with a new heart and a new Spirit. What we should not do is isolate John 3 from the rest of Scripture. Jesus is announcing the cleansing and Spirit- given life that only God can produce. “Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean... A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you.” EZEKIEL 36:25-26 When the gospel is preached in Acts, repentance, faith, water baptism, and receiving the Holy Ghost belong together in the life of discipleship. Peter said, “Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.” Baptism Is Not a Substitute for Faith Baptism matters because Jesus commanded it and the apostles practiced it. It identifies the believer with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ. But salvation is not automatic because someone was placed in water as a baby or participated in a ceremony without personal faith. Water cannot replace Christ. Ritual cannot replace repentance. The New Testament call is to believe, repent, be baptized, and walk in the new life the Spirit gives. Romans 6 says we are buried with Him by baptism into death, that like as Christ was raised from the dead, even so we also should walk in newness of life. The sign points to a reality: the old rule is broken, and a new life has begun. Do not stop at a moment of prayer. Follow Christ. Be baptized. Receive the Holy Ghost. Learn His Word. Join the fellowship of believers. Let the new birth become visible in a new walk. DEGREE AND DECLARE I am born from above by the work of God. I will not trust an empty ceremony. I identify with Jesus Christ, I receive the life of the Spirit, and I will walk in newness of life. • • • CHAPTER SEVEN A New Creature and the Gift of Righteousness You are not going to become a new creature someday if you perform well enough. If you are in Christ, the new creation has already begun. The Christian life is learning to live out what God has already made true in Christ. “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.” 2 CORINTHIANS 5:17 This does not mean every habit disappears in one day. Growth is real and discipleship is necessary. But the center has changed. You do not grow toward becoming a new creation. You grow because new life has been given. Righteousness Is Counted to the Believer The Bible uses the language of imputation, counting, and reckoning. Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness. Paul then speaks of the blessedness of the man “unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works.” “For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.” ROMANS 4:3 “Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works.” ROMANS 4:6 This is right standing given by God, not self-righteousness manufactured by man. Christ knew no sin, yet He was made sin for us, “that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.” Your righteousness is in Him. That truth protects you from pride because you did not create it, and from despair because your past did not create it either. “For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.” 2 CORINTHIANS 5:21 Created for Good Works Already Prepared New creation does not produce passivity. Ephesians says we are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them. The word workmanship means something God has made. You are His work, and because He has made you new, there is a walk prepared for you. “For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.” EPHESIANS 2:10 Jesus told His disciples, “Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit.” He spoke those words directly to His disciples, but the principle of chosen fruitfulness continues in those who belong to Him: salvation brings us into a life that bears fruit. “Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain.” JOHN 15:16 You are made in the image of God, recreated in Christ, and called to begin living from what He has already given you. Stop speaking as though you are waiting to become what God says you are. Learn to walk it out. DEGREE AND DECLARE I am a new creature in Christ. Righteousness has been counted to me through faith, and my standing is in Christ. I am God’s workmanship, created for good works, chosen to bear fruit that remains. • • • CHAPTER EIGHT No Condemnation, Confession, and Cleansing Many believers are saved but still wake up as though the courtroom opens every morning and God is waiting to condemn them again. They know the language of forgiveness, but guilt still governs their relationship with God. The gospel must answer that bondage without pretending believers never sin. “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.” ROMANS 8:1 No condemnation does not mean sin has become unimportant. It means Christ has answered the sentence that stood against the believer, and the Spirit now leads him into a new way of life. Condemnation says, “You are your failure; hide from God.” Conviction says, “This is wrong; come into the light and let God correct and cleanse you.” The Believer Does Not Claim Sinless Perfection “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.” 1 JOHN 1:8 John does not teach the believer to deny the reality of sin. In the same paragraph he gives the answer: confession and cleansing. The Christian is not made secure by pretending he never fails. He is made secure by walking in the light and trusting the faithful work of Christ. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” 1 JOHN 1:9 Notice the words faithful and just. Forgiveness is not God acting carelessly about sin. The cross has already dealt with sin righteously. When we confess, we agree with God, come into the light, and receive the cleansing He promised. Right Standing Is a Gift, Not a Claim of Perfection This is where imputed righteousness must be understood correctly. To say we have been made the righteousness of God in Christ is not to say we have reached sinless maturity in conduct. It means God has granted us a right standing in Christ, and from that standing the Spirit trains us to live righteously. When you fail, do not make failure your identity. Do not call darkness your nature as though Christ did nothing. But do not hide sin either. Confess it. Receive cleansing. Get up. Keep walking after the Spirit. “My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous.” 1 JOHN 2:1 The goal is holiness. The provision is Christ. The believer does not live casually with sin, and neither does he live under continual condemnation. He has an Advocate, a cleansing blood, a faithful Father, and the Holy Ghost leading him forward. DEGREE AND DECLARE There is no condemnation for me in Christ Jesus. I will not hide sin, and I will not make sin my identity. I confess, I receive forgiveness and cleansing, and I continue walking after the Spirit. • • • CHAPTER NINE Grace: Favor, Power, and Enablement Grace is often defined as unmerited favor, and that is a helpful beginning. We receive what we did not earn. But if that is the only way we understand grace, we can miss how active grace is in the believer’s life. Grace not only welcomes us; grace works in us, teaches us, strengthens us, and enables us to do what God has called us to do. Grace Saves “For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God.” EPHESIANS 2:8 We begin by receiving. God owes us nothing, yet in Christ He gives forgiveness, righteousness, life, adoption, and access. Grace destroys boasting because the source is God. Grace Teaches “For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world.” TITUS 2:11-12 Grace is not permission to remain in the old life. Paul says grace teaches. The same grace that forgives becomes a teacher, training the believer to deny what belongs to the old life and to live differently now. Grace Enables Labor and Mighty Work “But by the grace of God I am what I am: and his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain; but I laboured more abundantly than they all: yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.” 1 CORINTHIANS 15:10 Look at Paul’s language. Grace did not make him passive. Grace enabled labor. He worked, but he refused to take the glory as though the strength originated in himself. He said the grace of God was with him. This is why you can be graced for an assignment. Grace can enable you to endure, to serve, to give, to preach, to build, to forgive, to lead, and to stand where natural strength would fail. God told Paul, “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.” “And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work.” 2 CORINTHIANS 9:8 All grace, all sufficiency, every good work. Grace has many functions in the believer’s life because God is not only interested in getting you through the door of salvation. He intends to work through you. So do not say, “I am saved by grace,” and then live as though everything after salvation depends on your natural ability. The life that grace begins, grace continues to supply. DEGREE AND DECLARE I am saved by grace, taught by grace, strengthened by grace, and enabled by grace. God is able to make all grace abound toward me so that I can abound to every good work He has prepared. • • • CHAPTER TEN Delivered from Darkness into His Kingdom Salvation is forgiveness, but it is also transfer. God did not forgive you and leave you standing under the same authority. Paul says the Father delivered us from the power of darkness and translated us into the kingdom of His dear Son. “Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son: In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins.” COLOSSIANS 1:13-14 Delivered. Translated. Redeemed. Forgiven. These are movement words. You have not only been pardoned where you stood; you have been moved. Ownership has changed. Authority has changed. Your relationship to darkness has changed. This is why salvation naturally opens the message of the Kingdom. The same Christ who saves you becomes your King. You are not rescued so that you can remain self-governed. You are brought under the loving rule of Jesus Christ. Not Empty Religion, but the Rule of God God did not create man for empty religion. James can speak of “pure religion” when faith becomes practical love and holiness, so our issue is not the word religion itself. Our issue is a form without life. Adam was created to live under God’s government. Jesus came announcing the Kingdom of God, and salvation brings us back under the rule of the King. The Kingdom is righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost. It changes how we think, desire, obey, forgive, use money, treat people, raise families, and carry authority. The government of God begins inwardly and then becomes visible outwardly. “For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost.” ROMANS 14:17 This book is focused on the gospel of salvation, so we will not develop the whole Kingdom message here. You will see much more on this in The Gospel of the Kingdom of God, available as a free resource through overcomersglobalnetwork.com. For now, remember this: salvation is not only an exit from darkness. It is an entrance into a Kingdom. Many believers can describe what they were saved from but cannot explain what they were saved into. The gospel must teach both. DEGREE AND DECLARE I have been delivered from the power of darkness and translated into the Kingdom of God’s dear Son. Jesus is not only my Savior; He is my King. I will learn to live under His government. • • • CHAPTER ELEVEN Eternal Life and a Spirit-Led Life Many people hear eternal life and think only about what happens after death. The resurrection and our future with God are glorious parts of the gospel, but Jesus defined eternal life as knowing God. Eternal life begins now because relationship begins now. “And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.” JOHN 17:3 Salvation is not simply a ticket to use when you die. It is reconciliation with God now. You pray because access has been opened. You worship because you have been brought near. You read the Word because the God who saved you now teaches you how to live. The Holy Ghost Makes the Life of Christ Present in Us “The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God.” ROMANS 8:16 The Holy Ghost bears witness to belonging, but He does more than reassure us. He leads, corrects, strengthens, reveals Christ, gives power to witness, and produces the character of Christ in the believer. A Spirit-Led Life “For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.” ROMANS 8:14 A Spirit-led life is better language than merely saying the believer has “spiritual fruit” as though the Spirit were an accessory. The Christian life is a life increasingly led by the Spirit. He teaches us to put to death the deeds of the body, renew the mind through the Word, and respond to the Father as sons rather than slaves. “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance.” GALATIANS 5:22-23 Paul says fruit, singular, describing the Spirit-produced character that grows in a life yielded to Him. We should not build a heavy doctrine on the grammar alone, but the picture is clear: one Spirit produces a Christlike life in many expressions. Assurance Without Carelessness God wants believers to know they have eternal life. First John says these things were written “that ye may know.” Assurance is not arrogance. It is taking God at His Word. At the same time, assurance does not make us careless. The person who belongs to God learns to walk in the light, receive correction, confess sin, and follow the Spirit. Your confidence is not in pretending you never failed. Your confidence is in Christ. When you stumble, run to Him, not away from Him. The shepherd who saved you is faithful, and the Spirit who began working in you is still leading you. DEGREE AND DECLARE I have eternal life in the Son. I know the Father through Jesus Christ, and I am led by the Spirit of God. I will walk in the light, receive correction, and allow the life of Christ to be formed in me. • • • CHAPTER TWELVE The Gospel Must Reach You, Your House, and the World The gospel must reach you before it can move through you. It is possible to know the language of salvation, attend church, and even tell other people what they should believe while never allowing the good news to become a living reality in your own heart and life. The gospel first calls you to Christ. Then, once it has taken hold of you, it begins moving outward into your house, your relationships, your community, and the world. Let the Gospel Reach You First Before you try to carry salvation to somebody else, allow the gospel to keep reaching you. Let it confront what you still excuse. Let it heal the places where condemnation has ruled you. Let it renew the way you think, the way you speak, the way you treat people, and the way you live at home. We are not preaching a message that we only know mentally. We are carrying a life we have received. “For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call.” ACTS 2:39 Peter had just preached Christ crucified and risen. The people were pricked in their heart and asked what they should do. Peter told them to repent, be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. Then he declared that the promise was not only for the people standing in front of him. It reached their children and those who were still afar off. This is important: the gospel does not stop at the first person who hears it. The promise reaches forward. It reaches your children. It reaches the people around you. It reaches people you have not met yet. But it must first become real in you. You cannot give somebody a living expression of a Christ you only know as information. The Promise Reaches Your Household “And they said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.” ACTS 16:31 Paul and Silas did not tell the jailer that his private faith would automatically save people who refused to believe. The next verse says they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all that were in his house. The promise opened the door for the whole house to hear, believe, and respond. So do not stop praying at “Lord, save me.” Begin to believe God for your house. Let your family see what Christ has changed in you. Pray for them by name. Speak the Word when the Holy Ghost gives you opportunity. Open your home to the gospel. Your house should become a place where Christ can be heard, where prayer can happen, where people can be healed, restored, discipled, and brought under the lordship of Jesus. When the Gospel Enters a House Cornelius gathered his relatives and close friends before Peter arrived, and the Holy Ghost fell on those who heard the Word. Lydia believed and opened her house. Crispus believed on the Lord with all his house. Zacchaeus heard Jesus declare, “This day is salvation come to this house.” Again and again, the gospel refused to stay private. A saved person should pray for family, live the gospel before them, apologize where necessary, show the fruit of Christ, and create opportunities for the Word to be heard. The people who know you best should be able to see the difference the Savior is making in you. House to House “And how I kept back nothing that was profitable unto you, but have shewed you, and have taught you publickly, and from house to house.” ACTS 20:20 The early church did not depend only on a platform. The Word moved publicly and house to house. The gospel belongs in the church gathering, but it also belongs at the table, in the living room, on the phone, in the workplace, in the neighborhood, and wherever people can hear about Christ. Sharing the Gospel in a Conversation Not every gospel message will happen behind a pulpit. Some of the most important preaching you will ever do will happen in a conversation: at a table, in a car, on the phone, at work, in a hospital room, or sitting with someone who finally asks you a real question about God. “Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus.” ACTS 8:35 Philip did not begin by forcing a prepared speech onto the Ethiopian man. He listened to what the man was reading, asked a question, heard where the confusion was, and then began at that same Scripture and preached Jesus. This is a pattern we can use. Listen first. Find out what the person actually believes and what they are asking. Start where they are, but do not leave them there. Bring the conversation to Christ. A gospel conversation can be simple. Ask a question such as, “What do you believe about Jesus?” or “Have you ever understood what the cross actually accomplished?” Listen to the answer. Then use Scripture. Explain what happened to man, who Jesus is, what He did through His death and resurrection, and what it means to repent, believe, and receive the new life He gives. Do not turn the conversation into a debate you are trying to win. You are not there to show how much Bible you know. You are there to reveal Christ. Speak plainly. Let the Holy Ghost help you know when to ask, when to explain, when to be quiet, and when to invite the person to respond. You can say something as simple as: “Can I tell you what Jesus did for me and show you from the Bible what He offers you?” From there, let the conversation breathe. The gospel is powerful enough to work without pressure. Preach with Power, Not Pressure “And my speech and my preaching was not with enticing words of man’s wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power: That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God.” 1 CORINTHIANS 2:4-5 Powerful preaching does not begin with volume. It begins with revelation and dependence on the Holy Ghost. The power is not emotional manipulation. The aim is not to force a reaction. The aim is to make Christ clear, speak to the conscience with truth and love, and give the hearer room to respond to God. “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me.” ACTS 1:8 The Holy Ghost is not an accessory to gospel preaching. Jesus connected witness with power. We need correct words, but we also need the Spirit of God making Christ living to the hearer. He gives boldness, conviction, wisdom, revelation, and, as He wills, demonstrations of His power that confirm the message of Jesus Christ. A strong salvation message keeps a few realities in front: what happened to man, who Jesus is, what the cross and resurrection accomplished, the blood that redeems, the call to repent and believe, the new life God gives, and the invitation to come under the lordship of Christ. Then disciple the person. Do not celebrate a decision and abandon the convert. Teach the Word. Help them pray. Lead them toward baptism. Teach them to hear and obey God. Bring them into fellowship. Let the gospel become a life that multiplies. DEGREE AND DECLARE The gospel has reached me, and it will not stop with me. I believe the promise of God for my house. I will pray, live, speak, and make room for the Word to reach my household. I am a witness of Jesus Christ, and by the power of the Holy Ghost I will carry the gospel into conversations, homes, communities, and wherever He sends me. • • • CONCLUSION Keep Christ at the Center We have covered many truths: creation, the fall, man’s need, the Messiah, the cross, the blood, grace, repentance, faith, confession, baptism, new birth, imputed righteousness, no condemnation, cleansing, the Kingdom, the Holy Ghost, the house, and the mission. But all of these truths meet in one Person: Jesus Christ. The gospel of salvation is not a ladder by which man climbs to God. It is the announcement that God has come to us in Christ. The sinner is called to turn, believe, receive, and follow. The believer is called to live out the new life he has already received and to carry that life to others. “Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.” HEBREWS 12:2 When you preach salvation, do not make it cold. You are speaking about rescue. You are speaking about the God of Israel keeping His promise, the eternal Word becoming flesh, the Lamb shedding His blood, a grave that could not hold Him, sinners being made righteous in Christ, sons receiving the Spirit, houses hearing the Word, and a Kingdom receiving people who were once under darkness. Do not make the preacher the hero. Do not make the church the hero. Do not make the sinner’s works the hero. Keep Christ in front. If people leave impressed with us but unclear about Jesus, we have missed the center. The message is still good news. It still has power. Christ still saves. His blood still cleanses. The Holy Ghost still gives life and power. And whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved. DEGREE AND DECLARE I look unto Jesus. He is the author and finisher of my faith. I will keep Christ at the center of my life, my teaching, and my preaching, and I will carry the gospel with the help of the Holy Ghost. • • • SHARING GUIDE Carry the Gospel Clearly You do not need a pulpit and you do not need to know every answer before you tell someone about Jesus. Most gospel sharing happens in ordinary conversations. Listen well, ask honest questions, keep Christ in front, let Scripture carry the weight, and trust the Holy Ghost to make the Word living. A Simple Gospel Flow 1. God created man for His image and glory. Genesis 1:27; Romans 3:23 2. Sin entered, and death passed upon humanity. Romans 5:12; Romans 6:23 3. Jesus is the promised Messiah and the eternal Word made flesh. Matthew 1:21; John 1:1, 14 4. Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again. 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 5. Redemption and forgiveness are through His blood. Ephesians 1:7; 1 Peter 1:18-19 6. The response is repentance toward God and living faith in Jesus Christ. Acts 20:21; Romans 10:9-10 7. In Christ the believer is born again, made new, and given right standing. John 3:3; 2 Corinthians 5:17, 21; Romans 4:6 8. The believer is delivered from darkness and brought into the Kingdom of the Son. Colossians 1:13-14 9. The Holy Ghost leads the new life and gives power to witness. Romans 8:14; Acts 1:8 A Prayer of Salvation These words are not a magic formula. Salvation is not produced by repeating a sentence while the heart remains untouched. But if you are turning to God and trusting Jesus Christ, you can express that faith in prayer: Heavenly Father, I come to You in the name of Jesus. I confess that I have sinned and fallen short of Your glory. I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, that He died for my sins, was buried, and that You raised Him from the dead. I turn from my old life and I turn to You. Jesus, I receive You as my Lord and Savior. Thank You for forgiving me, cleansing me by Your blood, making me a new creation, and giving me eternal life. Fill me with Your Holy Spirit and teach me to walk with You. In Jesus’ name, Amen. What Comes Next? Tell someone what Christ has done. Begin reading the Gospel of John. Pray every day. Connect with a Bible-believing fellowship. Be baptized in water if you have not been. Ask the Holy Ghost to lead you. Learn to obey the Word. And begin praying for your house, because the gospel that reached you was never meant to stop with you.
